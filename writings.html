The Feminine Inspiration in Sacred Art

by Mark Balma

From the earliest days of the Christian Faith women have been an integral part of the balance toward our spirituality. Mary Magdalene was one of Jesus’ most ardent disciples and was the first to encounter Jesus at the tomb. It seems revealing that Jesus relates to women in important moments in the New Testament, his mother, Mary at the marriage at Cana, the woman who was forgiven and washed his feet with her tears, the adulterous woman at the Temple, the woman that sought to touch his garment, the woman at the well, Mary and Martha, and finally with Mary Magdalene at the tomb at His resurrection.

Throughout the Old and New Testament women have taken courageous acts of faith along with men to demonstrate their courage and strength. Sometimes in ways that seem not based in action but inward reflection and contemplation. It is the feminine in art that makes our faith more approachable and touches the deepest part of our soul, for it is through the heart that true acceptance and understanding is achieved.

Throughout my journey as a painter it is the depiction of women that have allowed me to explore a deeper part of my faith. My current fresco project Women of Faith explore ten women from the Old and New Testament in the first series of frescoes in a church dedicated to this theme. One must go beyond the written word to explore the depth in the scripture of subtle ways their faith was expressed.

Currently I am also working on a triptych of paintings God is my Rudder, The Grace of God and Mary at the Tomb. Each one of these images explore the gentle yet accepting nature of how the quiet voice of the spirit guides and directs us throughout our lives. As if a part of our being was meant to need the quiet, still darkness to reach the deepest parts of our spirit, for it is through the embrace of this empty void that we are filled. In each of these works of quiet acceptance, faith in God’s protection, and courage to believe when all seems lost are the underlying inspirations.

One unique way that women have contributed to our faith is through the mystic interpretations of life. Clare of Assisi, Saint Scholastica, Hildegard of Bingen are some of those whose writing have helped us enter into the mysticism of our faith. Transcending the earthly and heavenly realm as if to offer a portal or window that we may pass. Their writings are not about conquering the outside world but through directing our inward voyage to understand the power and purpose of the inner heart and soul. It is through this journey that as a painter I am inspired, to express what lies deep within each of us and our everlasting spirit. That is why portraiture is my primary inspiration as I see and feel the need to express the spirit placed in human form. So as I portray a person in my painting I am seeking the true spirit that lay beneath the physical exterior and through the interaction of other elements of the composition a moment of spiritual truth can be discovered and shared.

One of my first commissions, as a young painter, was to paint St. Therese, the little flower, it had a profound effect even though I didn’t really understand at the time. Her steadfast determination and unwavering devotion allowed her to transform and transcend beyond the confinement of her bedridden body and cloister.

Working with the Clares in Assisi was an opportunity to understand the depth of commitment that they share in the solitude of prayer and quiet reflection. We worked together in creating a series of frescoes that spoke to the example Clare provides to modern life.

Mark Balma
